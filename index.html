In development

An end-to-end volatility trading system

A complete system built from scratch — options data ingestion and cleaning, surface construction, forecasting realised and implied volatility, position sizing under a risk budget, and execution modelled with real frictions. The intellectual spine is Euan Sinclair on the mechanics and economics of volatility trading, and Marcos López de Prado on the statistics of not fooling yourself.

The point of publishing it is not the equity curve. It’s the research process: an evaluation protocol fixed before results are looked at, deflated performance statistics reported next to raw ones, execution costs modelled rather than assumed away, and written post-mortems whenever the system loses money or a hypothesis dies.

In brief

Who is Brad Lasater?

Brad Lasater is an applied scientist and machine-learning engineer with ten years of production experience in machine learning, mathematical optimization, and state estimation. He has worked on auction design and real-time ranking at Nift, live-event demand forecasting at Ticketmaster, particle filters and sensor scheduling for space surveillance at Lockheed Martin, and discrete-event simulation for production planning at Boeing, where he was awarded U.S. Patent 10,204,323. He holds an M.Eng. in Systems Engineering from Arizona State University and a B.S. in Applied Mathematics from the University of Texas at Arlington, and served as a Sergeant and instructor in the United States Marine Corps.

He is currently building an end-to-end systematic volatility trading system from scratch — options data ingestion, arbitrage-free surface construction, volatility forecasting, position sizing, and execution with modelled frictions — and publishing the research process behind it, including an evaluation protocol committed in public before any results existed and an append-only live track record whose dates are enforced by continuous integration. He is seeking quantitative research and quantitative engineering roles in New York.
