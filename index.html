Estimating what uncertainty is worth, then trading the difference.

I build production decision systems for noisy, constrained environments. The work is versioned and evaluated under a protocol committed in public before any results existed, designed to separate genuine edge from backtest noise.

In development

An end-to-end volatility system, under active construction

A research and execution platform for defined-risk index-volatility strategies, beginning with SPX and XSP at roughly 7–45 days to expiry. The system links point-in-time options data, arbitrage-aware surface construction, physical-variance forecasts, risk-neutral variance extraction, a no-trade gate, Greek-constrained sizing, event-driven backtesting, and execution under observed spreads and slippage.

The evaluation rules are fixed before any results are inspected: every trial is logged, validation is purged and walk-forward, costs are modelled from executable quotes rather than assumed away, and live observations are append-only. Post-mortems are written whenever the system loses money or a hypothesis dies.

In brief

Who is Brad Lasater?

Brad Lasater is an applied scientist and machine-learning engineer with ten years of experience building production systems for probabilistic forecasting, state estimation, ranking, and constrained optimization. At Nift he owns modeling and optimization for a multi-brand marketplace serving millions of users. Previously he led live-event demand forecasting at Ticketmaster, developed particle-filter and sensor-scheduling algorithms for space surveillance at Lockheed Martin, and patented a stochastic production-planning model at Boeing, where he was awarded U.S. Patent 10,204,323. He holds an M.Eng. in Systems Engineering from Arizona State University and a B.S. in Applied Mathematics from the University of Texas at Arlington, and served as a Sergeant and instructor in the United States Marine Corps.

His current research focuses on systematic volatility and the variance risk premium. He is building an end-to-end research and execution platform for defined-risk SPX and XSP structures at roughly 7–45 days to expiry, covering point-in-time market data, volatility-surface construction, physical and risk-neutral variance estimation, signal validation, portfolio risk, execution, and live monitoring — with an evaluation protocol committed in public before any results existed and an append-only live record whose dates are enforced by continuous integration. He is pursuing quantitative research, research-engineering, and quantitative developer roles in New York.
