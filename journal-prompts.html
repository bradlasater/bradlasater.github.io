How to turn last night into a log entry

  1. Scan the table below. Pick at most one row — or none. The entry exists because something surprised you, not because it is morning. No surprise, no entry; a stale-looking log is only a problem if the dates promised a schedule, and yours don't.
  2. Copy the template: cp content/log/TEMPLATE.html.example content/log/YYYY-MM-DD-a-short-slug.html — the filename becomes the URL, so the slug is the finding, not the topic (2026-09-02-wildcard-ack-delivers-nothing.html, not 2026-09-02-websocket-work.html).
  3. Fill the meta block. title states the finding. summary is one sentence a stranger can parse out of context — it becomes the meta description, the feed summary, and the index card, so it is the highest-leverage sentence in the entry. Assume 45 seconds and no clicks.
  4. Write the four sections in order: What I expected → What happened → Diagnosis → What changed as a result. In the build phase, "expected" is an assumption about a vendor, an API, or the infrastructure — state it precisely enough that it could have been wrong. Diagnosis is the section a hiring manager actually reads; it is the one that cannot be faked.
  5. If the entry reports any result at all, add a <h2>What this shows and does not show</h2> — every bullet beginning "It does not show that…". Highest trust-per-word content available.
  6. Build and check: python3 scripts/build_site.py, open the generated log/<slug>.html, read it once as a stranger, then commit. The entry page, the index, the feed, and the sitemap all update from that one command.
The island test

Every <h2> section must make sense if it is the only thing a reader ever sees — retrieval engines return passages, not pages. And keep the numbers: "429s under burst; shared token bucket at 40 RPS" is citable, "rate limits were tricky" is not.

Delete on sight

proven · consistently · robust (unqualified) · proprietary · cutting-edge · passionate about · significant returns · battle-tested · alpha-generating · any Sharpe without a period, a cost assumption, and an interval.

Length

Build-phase entries: 600–1,200 words. Research posts (replication, results): 1,800–4,500 words with named baselines and a robustness section. Don't confuse the two — a build entry with a robustness section is padding; a results post without one is marketing.

Phrasing bank

  • "It does not show that ___ is useful on real data." — scope
  • "Necessary, but not sufficient." — calibrated confidence
  • "Should be read as an order of magnitude, not a point estimate." — uncertainty
  • "I would not defend it further than 'worth running properly.'" — calibration
  • "Is it possible that a weak edge existed but was hidden by imperfections in the data? Absolutely." — steelmanning the null
