Volatility System · Evaluation Protocol

How I intend to avoid fooling myself

This document is written before the results exist, and is versioned so that any later change to it is visible. That is the whole point: a backtest is only evidence if the rules for judging it were fixed in advance.

Why pre-commit to an evaluation protocol before running a backtest?

The failure mode in systematic trading research is not bad mathematics. It is a thousand small, individually reasonable decisions — a slightly different lookback, a filtered universe, an excluded month — each made after seeing what it does to the equity curve. The result is a strategy fitted to its own evaluation, which performs beautifully until the moment real money is behind it.

The defence is to fix the judging criteria first and count every decision made afterwards. Everything below is that commitment, stated in public so that departing from it is costly.

What data and universe does the system trade, and how is survivorship bias avoided?

Phase 1 trades defined-risk SPX and XSP structures at roughly 7–45 days to expiry, and every input is reconstructed as it stood at the decision timestamp rather than as it stands today. Three rules make that concrete:

  • Universe: defined-risk SPX and XSP structures at roughly 7–45 days to expiry. Both are European-style and cash-settled, which removes early assignment and share delivery from the Phase 1 risk surface. Liquidity screens are applied on the date of the decision rather than retrospectively; when the universe extends beyond the index complex, selecting today’s liquid names and testing them over history is survivorship bias wearing a lab coat.
  • Point-in-time discipline: every input must be as it was available at the decision timestamp — including dividends, splits, index membership, and the discount curve. Any field that cannot be reconstructed point-in-time is excluded from the model.
  • Quote hygiene: stale, crossed, and locked quotes dropped; static no-arbitrage bounds enforced; forward implied from put–call parity rather than taken from a vendor field.

How do you cross-validate a trading strategy without leaking the future?

Standard k-fold cross-validation is invalid on overlapping financial labels, because a position held for three weeks shares information with three weeks of neighbouring observations. This system uses purged and embargoed k-fold for model selection, walk-forward for the reported number, and a final held-out period used exactly once:

  • Purged k-fold with embargo. Options labels span time — a position held for three weeks overlaps three weeks of subsequent observations. Standard k-fold leaks that overlap directly into the validation set. Training observations whose label windows overlap the validation window are purged, and an embargo period after each validation fold is dropped entirely to handle serial correlation.
  • Walk-forward as the headline. Cross-validation is for model selection. The number that gets reported is walk-forward: train on the past, trade the next period, roll forward, never look back.
  • A held-out final period, untouched until the system is otherwise frozen, and used exactly once. If it disagrees with the development results, the development results were wrong.

What baselines must a volatility forecast beat before it is allowed to size a position?

A volatility model is never evaluated against zero. It is evaluated against the cheapest forecast that already works, and it must beat that forecast out of sample before it is allowed anywhere near a position. Four baselines are mandatory here:

  • Implied volatility itself as the forecast of realised volatility.
  • HAR-RV on high-frequency realised variance.
  • A GARCH-family conditional variance model.
  • For the strategy as a whole: naive short-variance carry, unconditionally sized.

A learned model that cannot beat HAR-RV is not a discovery. It is a defect, and it gets debugged rather than deployed.

How are execution costs and frictions modelled in an options backtest?

Fills are never assumed at the mid, because in options the spread is frequently larger than the entire modelled edge. Every backtested trade in this system is charged for the following, at minimum:

  • Half-spread crossing, scaled by quoted width at the decision time and by order size relative to displayed depth.
  • Commissions and exchange fees per contract.
  • Legging risk on multi-leg structures.
  • Delta-hedging costs at the actual rebalancing frequency, including the hedge’s own spread.
  • Early-assignment and borrow costs, which do not apply to the European, cash-settled Phase 1 universe and are modelled only when the system extends to American-style products.
  • Margin requirements, modelled well enough to know whether a drawdown becomes a forced liquidation.

Every reported result states its assumed fill convention. A strategy whose edge disappears under a realistic spread assumption is reported as such rather than quietly re-run at the mid.

Which performance statistics are reported with every result?

Every result published from this system reports seven statistics together: raw Sharpe, deflated Sharpe, the trial count behind them, the probability of backtest overfitting, maximum drawdown and time to recover, turnover and cost share of gross P&L, and return attribution by Greek. A raw Sharpe ratio on its own is not a result — without knowing how many configurations produced it, it is uninterpretable.

What pre-committed conditions retire a strategy?

Kill criteria are fixed in advance, because the temptation to keep adjusting a losing strategy is the expensive one and it is strongest exactly when judgement is worst. Any one of the following retires a strategy from this system to the research log rather than the portfolio:

  • Deflated Sharpe not distinguishable from zero at the chosen confidence level.
  • Live out-of-sample performance below the lower bound of the backtest’s predicted range for a pre-set window.
  • Realised costs exceeding modelled costs by more than a pre-set margin — the model was wrong about the world, whatever the P&L says.
  • Return attribution showing the P&L came from an exposure the strategy was not intended to take, even if that P&L is positive.

That last one matters most. A strategy that makes money for reasons other than its thesis is not a working strategy; it is an unmeasured risk that has not yet been paid for.

What gets published, including the failures?

Publication is not selective, because a research record that only contains successes is indistinguishable from one that has been curated after the fact. Four things go up regardless of how they reflect on the system:

  • Every result with its full statistics table, never a headline number alone.
  • Every dead hypothesis, with what was expected and what actually happened.
  • Every material change to this protocol, dated, with the reasoning.
  • Trade-level logs behind any live track record, so the reported statistics can be recomputed independently.

This protocol is versioned in the repository. Changes are commits, not edits — last revised August 24, 2026.
