In development

The volatility system

Options are a market in forecasts. The trade is the forecast error.

An end-to-end research and execution platform for defined-risk index-volatility strategies, under active construction and documented as it goes. Phase 1 focuses on defined-risk SPY and SPX/SPXW structures at roughly 5–45 days to expiry, with VIX options captured for the term structure. The architecture is designed to generalise to additional underlyings and strategy families once the initial research and execution loop is validated. What I own at every stage below is the interface, the validation, and the failure handling — not necessarily the implementation. PyArrow, NumPy and SciPy do the work they are good at; the pricing identities, the entitlement probes and the coverage audits are mine because they are what decides whether a number is trustworthy.

01 · Premise

Why volatility

An option price is a statement about the distribution of future returns. Implied volatility is that statement made legible. It is also, persistently and measurably, a biased forecast: implied volatility exceeds subsequently realised volatility most of the time in index options, because someone is being paid to carry the risk of it not doing so.

That premium is well documented, which means it is not an edge by itself. Harvesting it naively is a strategy that makes money for years and then returns all of it in a fortnight. The interesting questions are the ones underneath: when is the premium unusually rich relative to the risk being assumed, how much should be risked given a specific portfolio of Greeks, and what does the position actually cost to put on and hedge after spreads, slippage, and hedging costs.

Those are estimation and optimisation problems under uncertainty — the discipline I have spent a decade applying in other domains. Why this domain, and why now, is on the homepage.

Trading is a game of edges, and the edge is usually in the execution and the risk management rather than the prediction. — the working assumption, after Euan Sinclair
02 · Architecture

System stages

Each stage is independently testable and independently loggable. Failures are attributed to a stage, not to “the model.” Two of the nine stages run today; the rest are designed and not built, and each card says which it is. Where a stage is partly blocked by the data entitlement rather than by unwritten code, it says that too.

Data & cleaning

Operational

The ingestion layer takes end-of-day and intraday options chains, option trades and day bars, underlying prices, dividend schedules, and the Treasury curve, on a schedule with a daily coverage audit that reports on the absence of a job rather than only on its failures.

What cleaning can do on this data tier: filter by open interest, flag quotes that violate static no-arbitrage bounds, and reconstruct the forward from put–call parity rather than trusting a vendor field — which is necessary here rather than merely preferable, because index levels are not entitled.

What it cannot, yet: option NBBO is not entitled, so there is no bid, ask or mid anywhere in the warehouse. Every price is a traded price — last trade, else day close. Dropping crossed and locked quotes and filtering by spread width are all defined in terms of fields this tier does not provide, and are blocked behind a quote entitlement.

ATM term structure

Operational

Implied volatility is inverted per contract from the traded price — Newton with bounds, Brent fallback, an error rather than a NaN when the price sits outside the discounted no-arbitrage bounds — and reduced to an ATM term structure: one row per date, root and expiry, carrying the parity forward, the ATM strike and both leg IVs.

It is built from option day bars rather than chain snapshots, because day bars reach back to 2022-08-31 while the snapshot sweep only begins in 2026. Pricing is done in the forward measure, which reduces Black–Scholes–Merton to Black-76 — a spot and a dividend yield are exactly what this tier does not provide.

Smile & surface

Planned

The ATM curve gives the level and slope of at-the-money volatility. It says nothing off-strike or between expiries, because there is no smile fit and no interpolator — the single largest gap between what runs and what the rest of this architecture assumes.

The design is a parametric fit in total implied variance, SVI per slice moving toward SSVI for a globally consistent surface, with calendar-spread and butterfly no-arbitrage constraints enforced on the fit rather than checked afterwards, so that downstream Greeks are internally consistent. None of it is written yet.

Volatility forecasting

Planned

Forecasting starts from realised volatility estimators that use the full bar (Parkinson, Garman–Klass, Yang–Zhang) rather than close-to-close, feeding HAR-RV and GARCH-family baselines. Learned models must beat those baselines out of sample before they are allowed anywhere near a position — a machine-learning model that cannot outperform HAR-RV is not a finding, it is a bug.

Signal & sizing

Planned

The spread between forecast realised and market implied volatility becomes a signal only after it clears modelled costs. Sizing separates direction from conviction via meta-labelling: one model decides whether to act, a second sizes the bet, and the portfolio is constrained by vega, gamma, and net-liquidation budgets rather than by notional.

Risk & hedging

Planned

Risk management runs on continuous Greek accounting, with discrete delta-hedging against an explicit rebalancing band (hedging error is a cost to be optimised, not a nuisance), scenario and stress grids across spot and vol shocks, and margin modelling — because the strategy that survives a drawdown on paper can still be closed out by a margin call.

Execution

Planned

Execution models fills at achievable prices rather than at the mid, because in options the quoted spread is frequently wider than the modelled edge. Multi-leg structures carry legging risk, and index settlement carries hazards of its own — AM-settled SPX expiries print off the opening auction, not the close. Early assignment, stock borrow, and share delivery do not apply to SPX/SPXW (European, cash-settled), but they do apply to SPY (American-style, physically settled) and are therefore first-class Phase 1 execution risks. Any backtest that quietly assumes mid fills is measuring the size of its own assumption.

Evaluation

Planned

Evaluation uses purged and embargoed cross-validation, walk-forward testing, and deflated Sharpe ratios reported alongside raw ones, and an explicit count of every configuration tried so that the probability of backtest overfitting can be estimated rather than ignored.

Operations & logging

Planned

Operations means structured logs at every stage, daily reconciliation of expected against actual positions and P&L, and an alerting layer for silent failures — a stale data feed that produces plausible-looking numbers is far more dangerous than one that crashes.

03 · Results

Track record

Nothing is published here until the evaluation protocol has been run end-to-end and the out-of-sample clock has been running long enough for the number to mean something.

The live record has its own page, and its contract is already in force: append-only, each observation a separate dated commit, with CI rejecting any edit to a day that has already been published. No observations have been recorded yet — the record starts when the first stage trades. Every statistic on it is computed in the browser from the raw data file.

It reports the minimum track record length next to the Sharpe ratio — how long the record would have to run before that Sharpe could be told apart from luck. While it is too short to mean anything, the page says so.

04 · Failures

Post-mortems

The log is empty today because the system has not yet produced a result worth writing about. When it has, those will be the most useful pages here and the hardest ones to fake.
