The volatility system

Options are a market in forecasts. The trade is the forecast error.

An end-to-end research and execution platform for defined-risk index-volatility strategies, under active construction and documented as it goes. Phase 1 focuses on defined-risk SPX and XSP structures at roughly 7–45 days to expiry. The architecture is designed to generalise to additional underlyings and strategy families once the initial research and execution loop is validated. Every stage below is something I am building myself rather than importing.

01 · Premise

Why volatility

An option price is a statement about the distribution of future returns. Implied volatility is that statement made legible. It is also, persistently and measurably, a biased forecast: implied volatility exceeds subsequently realised volatility most of the time in index options, because someone is being paid to carry the risk of it not doing so.

That premium is well documented, which means it is not an edge by itself. Harvesting it naively is a strategy that makes money for years and then returns all of it in a fortnight. The interesting questions are the ones underneath: when is the premium unusually rich relative to the risk being assumed, how much should be risked given a specific portfolio of Greeks, and what does the position actually cost to put on and hedge after spreads, slippage, and hedging costs.

Those are estimation and optimisation problems under uncertainty — which is what I have spent a decade doing in other domains. The system below is my attempt to do it properly in this one.

Trading is a game of edges, and the edge is usually in the execution and the risk management rather than the prediction. — the working assumption, after Euan Sinclair
02 · Architecture

System stages

Each stage is independently testable and independently loggable. Failures are attributed to a stage, not to “the model.” Status labels are current: what runs today, what is being checked, and what is designed but not yet built.

Data & cleaning

Operational

The ingestion layer takes end-of-day and intraday options chains, underlying prices, dividend schedules, and the discount curve. Cleaning is the unglamorous half of the edge: dropping stale and crossed quotes, filtering by open interest and spread width, reconstructing the forward from put–call parity rather than trusting a vendor field, and flagging quotes that violate static no-arbitrage bounds.

Surface construction

Under validation

Surface construction inverts quotes to implied volatility, then fits a parametric surface in total implied variance — SVI per slice, moving toward SSVI for a globally consistent surface. Calendar-spread and butterfly no-arbitrage constraints are enforced on the fit, not checked afterwards, so downstream Greeks are internally consistent.

Volatility forecasting

Planned

Forecasting starts from realised volatility estimators that use the full bar (Parkinson, Garman–Klass, Yang–Zhang) rather than close-to-close, feeding HAR-RV and GARCH-family baselines. Learned models must beat those baselines out of sample before they are allowed anywhere near a position — a machine-learning model that cannot outperform HAR-RV is not a finding, it is a bug.

Signal & sizing

Planned

The spread between forecast realised and market implied volatility becomes a signal only after it clears modelled costs. Sizing separates direction from conviction via meta-labelling: one model decides whether to act, a second sizes the bet, and the portfolio is constrained by vega, gamma, and net-liquidation budgets rather than by notional.

Risk & hedging

Planned

Risk management runs on continuous Greek accounting, with discrete delta-hedging against an explicit rebalancing band (hedging error is a cost to be optimised, not a nuisance), scenario and stress grids across spot and vol shocks, and margin modelling — because the strategy that survives a drawdown on paper can still be closed out by a margin call.

Execution

Planned

Execution models fills at achievable prices rather than at the mid, because in options the quoted spread is frequently wider than the modelled edge. Multi-leg structures carry legging risk, and index settlement carries hazards of its own — AM-settled SPX expiries print off the opening auction, not the close. Early assignment, stock borrow, and share delivery do not apply to European, cash-settled index options; they become first-class risks only when the system extends to American-style products. Any backtest that quietly assumes mid fills is measuring the size of its own assumption.

Evaluation

Planned

Evaluation uses purged and embargoed cross-validation, walk-forward testing, and deflated Sharpe ratios reported alongside raw ones, and an explicit count of every configuration tried so that the probability of backtest overfitting can be estimated rather than ignored.

Operations & logging

Planned

Operations means structured logs at every stage, daily reconciliation of expected against actual positions and P&L, and an alerting layer for silent failures — a stale data feed that produces plausible-looking numbers is far more dangerous than one that crashes.

03 · Results

Track record

Nothing is published here until the evaluation protocol has been run end-to-end and the out-of-sample clock has been running long enough for the number to mean something.

The live record has its own page. It is append-only: each observation is a separate dated commit, and CI rejects any edit to a day that has already been published, so the elapsed time is verifiable rather than asserted. Every statistic on it is computed in the browser from the raw data file.

It reports the minimum track record length next to the Sharpe ratio — how long the record would have to run before that Sharpe could be told apart from luck. While it is too short to mean anything, the page says so.

What this is built on

The mechanics and economics: where the premium comes from, why hedging error matters as much as the forecast, and how position sizing rather than prediction separates traders who survive from those who do not.
