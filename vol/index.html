The volatility system

Options are a market in forecasts. The trade is the forecast error.

An end-to-end systematic volatility trading system, built from scratch and documented as it goes. Every stage below is something I’m building myself rather than importing: options data ingestion and cleaning, arbitrage-free surface construction, volatility forecasting, position sizing against a risk budget, execution with modelled frictions, and an evaluation layer designed to make the results hard to believe rather than easy.

01 · Premise

Why volatility

An option price is a statement about the distribution of future returns. Implied volatility is that statement made legible. It is also, persistently and measurably, a biased forecast: implied volatility exceeds subsequently realised volatility most of the time, across most underlyings, because someone is being paid to carry the risk of it not doing so.

That premium is well documented, which means it is not an edge by itself. Harvesting it naively is a strategy that makes money for years and then returns all of it in a fortnight. The interesting questions are the ones underneath: when is the premium unusually rich relative to the risk being assumed, how much should be risked given a specific portfolio of Greeks, and what does the position actually cost to put on and hedge after spreads, slippage, and assignment risk.

Those are estimation and optimisation problems under uncertainty — which is what I have spent a decade doing in other domains. The system below is my attempt to do it properly in this one.

Trading is a game of edges, and the edge is usually in the execution and the risk management rather than the prediction. — the working assumption, after Euan Sinclair
02 · Architecture

System stages

Each stage is independently testable and independently loggable. Failures are attributed to a stage, not to “the model.”

Data & cleaning

End-of-day and intraday options chains, underlying prices, dividend schedules, and the discount curve. The cleaning layer is the unglamorous half of the edge: dropping stale and crossed quotes, filtering by open interest and spread width, reconstructing the forward from put–call parity rather than trusting a vendor field, and flagging quotes that violate static no-arbitrage bounds.

Surface construction

Implied volatility inversion, then a parametric fit in total implied variance (SVI per-slice, moving toward SSVI for a globally consistent surface). Calendar-spread and butterfly no-arbitrage constraints are enforced on the fit, not checked afterwards, so downstream Greeks are internally consistent.

Volatility forecasting

Realised volatility estimators that use the full bar (Parkinson, Garman–Klass, Yang–Zhang) rather than close-to-close, feeding HAR-RV and GARCH-family baselines. Learned models must beat those baselines out of sample before they are allowed anywhere near a position — a machine-learning model that cannot outperform HAR-RV is not a finding, it is a bug.

Signal & sizing

The spread between forecast realised and market implied volatility becomes a signal only after it clears modelled costs. Sizing separates direction from conviction via meta-labelling: one model decides whether to act, a second sizes the bet, and the portfolio is constrained by vega, gamma, and net-liquidation budgets rather than by notional.

Risk & hedging

Continuous Greek accounting, discrete delta-hedging with an explicit rebalancing band (hedging error is a cost to be optimised, not a nuisance), scenario and stress grids across spot and vol shocks, and margin modelling — because the strategy that survives a drawdown on paper can still be closed out by a margin call.

Execution

Fills are modelled at achievable prices, not at the mid. Multi-leg structures carry legging risk; American options carry early-assignment risk; short options carry borrow and pin risk. Any backtest that quietly assumes mid fills is measuring the size of its own assumption.

Evaluation

Purged and embargoed cross-validation, walk-forward testing, deflated Sharpe ratios reported alongside raw ones, and an explicit count of every configuration tried so that the probability of backtest overfitting can be estimated rather than ignored.

Operations & logging

Structured logs at every stage, daily reconciliation of expected against actual positions and P&L, and an alerting layer for silent failures — a stale data feed that produces plausible-looking numbers is far more dangerous than one that crashes.

03 · Results

Track record

Nothing is published here until the evaluation protocol has been run end-to-end and the out-of-sample clock has been running long enough for the number to mean something.

The live record has its own page. It is append-only: each observation is a separate dated commit, and CI rejects any edit to a day that has already been published, so the elapsed time is verifiable rather than asserted. Every statistic on it is computed in the browser from the raw data file.

It reports the minimum track record length next to the Sharpe ratio — how long the record would have to run before that Sharpe could be told apart from luck. While it is too short to mean anything, the page says so.
