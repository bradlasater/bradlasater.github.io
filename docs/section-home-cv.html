Homepage, CV & 404

living document · maintain by hand
index.html · cv.html · 404.html

Purpose

The recruiting surface. These three pages are what a hiring manager, recruiter, or future collaborator actually sees: the homepage pitches the volatility build and the research-to-production narrative, the CV carries the full record, and the 404 salvages anyone who follows a stale link. Everything here is hand-authored static HTML — no template, no generator — which makes it the fastest part of the site to change and the easiest to quietly break.

Key files

How it works

Homepage structure. A single-column page of <section class="shell section"> blocks, each with an eyebrow, an h2.section__title, and an aria-labelledby hook. The hero leads with name, role line, a one-sentence thesis, and two bio paragraphs, then a primary action pair ("See the volatility system", "Full CV"). After that: a .feature block on the system under construction, the "Why volatility, why now" rationale (the argument that past work and volatility trading are the same estimation problem), selected experience as .role articles, credentials as a .deflist, availability, and contact. The contact section carries the build-stamped "last updated" line.

CV structure. Same chrome, plus a .hero--cv with a <picture> portrait (320w/720w WebP srcset over headshot.jpg, explicit dimensions, fetchpriority="high"). Six roles instead of four, education and recognition as .deflists, and a three-group .taggroup skills block. The JSON-LD is a near-copy of the homepage graph (same #brad Person node) plus a BreadcrumbList — keep the two files' structured data in sync when job titles, the seeks demand, or the bio change.

404. Reuses the hero classes, then adds assets/css/error.css for the framed assets/deja.jpg figure — the astronaut alone in a field, "somewhere off the planned trajectory". The source image is only 332×152, so the CSS fades the frame edges into the page background to read as cinematic rather than pixelated, and the og:image points at it anyway (audit item 38 wants a 1200×630 variant). Four action buttons cover everything actually on the site.

Design system. assets/css/site.css is dark-only (color-scheme: dark) with OKLCH tokens: cool neutral ramps at hue 255, a blue accent at hue 232, and teal/warm-red semantic colours chosen to stay separable for deuteranopic viewers (always paired with a sign or label, never colour alone). Text is deliberately never pure white — halation makes it harder to read on dark. Fonts are Inter (sans), Newsreader (serif) and JetBrains Mono, all from Google Fonts with preconnect hints. Type is a fluid --step-* clamp scale across 320–1280px viewports; spacing is a 4pt --sp-* scale; content shells are 72rem (48rem narrow). The header collapses to a stacked, non-sticky layout at max-width: 56rem — that number was measured in the browser (seven nav items plus the brand fit at 880px and wrap at 850px), so re-measure it whenever a nav item is added or renamed.

Analytics. assets/js/analytics.js injects GoatCounter (site code bradlasater) with a guard flag so a double include cannot double-count. It refuses to run on localhost/127.0.0.1 and honours Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control. No cookies, no personal data, hence no consent banner. Beyond pageviews it records two custom events, because the question that matters for a job search is whether anyone who landed went on to act: contact-email on mailto clicks, and outbound-<hostname> on external links. Count failures are swallowed — analytics must never break the page.

Conventions and gotchas

  • External links open in a new tab. Every off-site anchor carries target="_blank" rel="noopener"; where the target is also an identity claim (LinkedIn, sometimes GitHub) the tokens merge into rel="me noopener" — do not drop the me, it pairs with the <link rel="me"> tags in the head. Internal links stay same-tab.
  • Accessibility is structural, not bolted on. Every page opens with a .skip-link to #main, and <main id="main" tabindex="-1"> so the skip target can take focus. Focus is a :focus-visible box-shadow ring, with a forced-colors media query that restores a real outline because box-shadow is not painted in Windows High Contrast. Nav marks the current page with aria-current="page".
  • Do not hand-edit the date stamps. The "This page was last updated" line and the JSON-LD dateModified on index.html and cv.html are rewritten by scripts/build_site.py from the file's last commit, excluding the build's own "chore: rebuild derived site metadata" commits (without that exclusion the stamp loop would never reach a fixed point). The pre-commit hook regenerates them; CI fails the push if they go stale. A hand-typed date is treated as an incentive to lie — that is the whole point of the mechanism.
  • British spelling in visible prose ("optimisation", "modelling"), but og:description and the JSON-LD blocks use American spelling with inLanguage: "en-US" / og:locale en_US. This was a deliberate nod to US recruiters, it has already caused two regressions (2026-09-01, commits 93a4286 and 455d6e4), and whether to keep or unify it is an open question — audit item 33. Until that is decided, match the split exactly when editing.
  • The homepage and CV JSON-LD are hand-duplicated. The Person, patent CreativeWork and ProfilePage nodes exist in both files; the CV adds a BreadcrumbList. Nothing keeps them in sync except you.
  • llms.txt is prose, not generated. If you reposition the site (new target roles, new sections), rewrite it by hand — only the log-entries block between the BUILD markers is machine-owned.
